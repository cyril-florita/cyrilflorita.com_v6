# Kord pitch (`/pitch/kord/`)

Kord is a fictional online-only road-bike brand, the fourth design and dev pitch (in progress, started 2026-11-21). Inspiration boards are in `new-work/cadence/` (git-ignored): dark studio product sites with one bright accent, a giant word behind the bike, hotspots and exploded views. The user chose a chartreuse accent, a 3D bike modelled in Blender, and online only (no physical shop). Noindex and not in the sitemap for now. **Renamed from Cadence to Kord on 2026-11-23** (the user's choice): routes, folders and copy say Kord; the CSS prefix `cd-` is left over from the first name (like Viteo's `dl-`), and the git-ignored `new-work/cadence/` folder (inspiration, the donor model, the HDRIs) keeps its name. Stealth is the default paint, first in `PAINTS` (Volt was, briefly, on 2026-11-23); the hero poster and the Arc card are Stealth, the Atlas card Volt. **Logo** (2026-11-23, the user's pick from a sheet of stretched type): the mark plus "KORD" in Archivo Expanded Black (Archivo at width 125, weight 900) stretched 2.2× wide and 0.8× tall — an outlined SVG path (`components/kord/wordmark.js`, generated by `scripts/kord/make_wordmark.py`, so it doesn't depend on a font), 11 px tall in the header and footer (9 px ≤560px, so the header's button stays on one line); the same stretched type is the frame's wordmark. The fixed header turns solid over the first screen of scrolling (a CSS scroll-driven animation) so text doesn't show through it. **Headings are uppercase** (2026-11-23, the user's call, after a pass with all Archivo text in caps): h1–h3, the 3D hotspot and callout titles and the Find your bike questions (one rule in kord.css); running text, nav and labels keep their case. Buttons (`.cd-btn`) and call-to-action links (`.cd-card-build`, the arrowed "Build yours" / "Try the virtual fit") are uppercase too since 2026-11-24 (the user's call), tracked 0.04em; plain underlined text links (Start over, Copy a link…) keep their case. The hero headline (`line` on the Arc in `BIKES`) is "Refined speed. Less weight, more substance." (2026-11-24, the user's copy, in sentence case; before that, briefly, "Refined Speed—Less Weight, More Substance.", and originally "Speed you can hold."). BikeStory breaks it after the first sentence (or a dash), keeping that line's last word with its punctuation (`.cd-nowrap`), so it reads REFINED SPEED. / LESS WEIGHT, MORE SUBSTANCE. The first sentence is 25% bigger than the rest and on its own line with a little space under it (`.cd-h1-lead`: `display: block`, 1.25em, `margin-bottom: 0.22em`; the user's calls), and the base is sized so it still fits on one line — `clamp(30px, 3.3vw, 48px)` in a `min(640px, 45vw)` column on desktop, `clamp(22px, 6.1vw, 34px)` on phones; measured on one line at 360, 390, 820, 900, 1100, 1280, 1440 and 1920px. On short phones (≤700px tall) the hero's description is hidden so the copy stays clear of the bike (it was the paint swatches, before they moved to the top).

## The bike (`build_bike.py`)

```
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b -P scripts/kord/build_bike.py -- <out_dir> --donor new-work/cadence/road_bike/scene.gltf --ao [--bike arc|crest|meridian|atlas] [--lite 0.45] [--glb public/pitch/kord/models/<id>.glb] [--stills <dir> --pathtrace --hdri new-work/cadence/hdri/studio_dark_1k.hdr] [--preview]
```

**The site's bike is a hybrid (since 2026-11-23).** Its frame, fork, cockpit (stem, bar, levers and hoods), seatpost and saddle come from **"Road Bike" by tanity**, CC BY 4.0, https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/road-bike-cb03b0657fbc4693b59194124ba713db (commercial use allowed; the credit is in the page footer — keep it). That model is unfinished (one chainring, no derailleurs, rim brakes, 32-spoke box rims), so the wheels (rims, spokes, hubs, rotors), drivetrain, disc brakes and wordmark are still ours. `--donor` imports it, maps its axles onto ours (scale 0.427: wheelbase 990 mm, which lands its bottom bracket within a millimetre of ours), keeps only the meshes listed in `DONOR_PARTS` (each with the material it takes; everything else — its wheels, chainring, chain, rim brakes, outside cables and their guides — is dropped), joins them into our part names, and measures its down tube by ray casts so the wordmark (36 mm instead of 46) wraps onto it. Its frame is two-tone: paint in a band along each tube, raw carbon (`Carbon`) on the edges, and the wordmark and rim stripes sit on carbon, so every paint's decal is light. Its seat tube is where ours is at the front derailleur's height, so the derailleur didn't move. The pedals are the donor's too (since 2026-11-23, at the user's request): its level non-drive platform pedal (dark grey cage, amber reflectors — materials `Pedal` and `Reflector`), its spindle's inner end set on our crank arms' outer faces, mirrored for the drive side, and added to the Crankset so they turn with it. So are the tires (also 2026-11-23, at the user's request): its tan gumwall (`Gumwall`) and molded tread replace our Tire/TireWall faces on both wheels and sit on our rims as they are (same axles, same 700×28 size); the tread is modelled block by block (~86k triangles a tire), so it's decimated to 15% (`--tread <ratio>`; 0.3 shows a little more pattern but makes the GLB 925 KB instead of 697 KB). Without `--donor` the script still builds the all-procedural bike described below (frame and fork voxel-remeshed from swept tubes, its own cockpit and saddle). Our first all-procedural version was compared side by side with the donor before the switch; the donor's frame, levers and saddle were clearly better, our wheels and drivetrain clearly better.

Ours is built from nothing in metres (X forward, Y across, Z up; the drive side is −Y, the rider's right — it's built on +Y and mirrored at the end). A 56 cm race geometry: 990 mm wheelbase, 410 mm chainstays, 73°/73.5° head/seat angles, stack 570 / reach 390, 700×28 tires.

- **Frame and fork**: tubes swept with superellipse/truncated-airfoil sections, then voxel-remeshed (2.4 mm) and smoothed so the joints blend like a monocoque, then decimated.
- **Wheels**: lathed tire (tread and sidewall materials), 50 mm U-shaped rim (its decal stripe is off since 2026-11-23, `RIM_STRIPE`), hub, 24 bladed spokes, 160 mm rotor; the rear carries an 11–34 12-speed cassette.
- **Drivetrain**: 52/36 rings, cranks and pedals; the chain is a real path (outer and inner tangents round the ring, a 14T cog and the two jockey wheels) resampled at 12.7 mm pitch into links; rear and front derailleurs.
- **Cockpit**: one rotation-minimising sweep per side, flat aero tops morphing into a round taped drop (no pinch or twist), hoods, levers, a head unit.
- **Wordmark**: "KORD" in Archivo Expanded Black stretched 2.2× wide and 0.8× tall like the logo (44 mm type), triangulated and subdivided to ~3.5 mm so the shrinkwrap can follow the down tube.
- **Head badge** (2026-11-23; horizontal since 2026-11-24, the user's call): the model name (`BADGE` = "ARC™", the ™ in JetBrains Mono, see above) across the front of the head tube, reading left to right seen from the front, in Archivo Expanded Black unstretched — 34 mm wide with the ™ (capped at `0.034`). It ran down the tube, one letter under another, until then. Its width is the part of the tube's front that faces forward, measured by ray casts at mid height (out to where the surface has curved 8 mm back: ~37 mm on the donor frame's aero head tube; the profile is printed), capped at 30 mm, so the letters don't wrap round the sides. It has its own material, **Badge**, colored per paint to contrast with it (`badge` in `PAINTS`: chartreuse on Stealth, near-black on Volt and Glacier, cream on Ember) — the head tube's front is paint, so the shared Decal color vanished on Volt and nearly on Glacier (the down-tube wordmark sits on raw carbon and keeps Decal). Projected onto the tube like the wordmark, then merged into the Wordmark object as its second material, so it stays with the frame when the bike comes apart. `MAT["Badge"]` has a fake user, or the donor import's cleanup of unused materials deletes it.
- Objects (each an exploded-view part, origin at its pivot): Frame, Fork, Wheel_F, Wheel_R, Cockpit, Seatpost, Saddle, Crankset, Chain, RearDerailleur, FrontDerailleur, Brake_F, Brake_R, Wordmark. Materials: Paint, Carbon, Decal, Badge, Tire, TireWall, Metal, Silver, Rotor, Tape, Hood, Saddle, Rubber, Pedal, Reflector, Gumwall — a new one needs adding in `mats` in BikeStory.js too.
- Hybrid: ~165k triangles, 750 KB as Draco GLB with the baked AO (the all-procedural bike was ~188k, 666 KB). `--stills` renders the transparent hero poster and one still per range bike in its paint (the concept uses the one frame for all four bikes).

## Four models (2026-11-24)

Until 2026-11-24 one frame stood in for all four bikes. Now `build_bike.py --bike <id>` builds each model from the same parts, with its differences taken from data.js (`VARIANTS` at the top of the script):

| Model | Rims | Tires | Frame | Head badge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arc™ | 50 mm | 28 mm | as the donor | ARC™ (letters 5.5 mm) |
| Crest™ | 35 mm | 28 mm | compact: the seat cluster 35 mm lower, more seatpost showing | CREST™ (3.9 mm) |
| Meridian™ | 40 mm | 32 mm | front end 25 mm taller (its extra stack), cockpit raised with it | MERIDIAN™ (2.8 mm) |
| Atlas™ | 30 mm | 38 mm | front end 20 mm taller | ATLAS™ (3.9 mm) |

- **Rims**: our 50 mm profile scaled from the bead inward (`rim`); the spokes end just inside the new base.
- **Tires**: the donor's 28 mm tire scaled about the bead, across and outward (`tire / 28`); wider tires lift the whole bike (`LIFT`: Meridian 4 mm, Atlas 10 mm) so they stand on the floor.
- **Axles** (since 2026-11-24): each model's chainstays and wheelbase (`cs`, `wb`, from DETAILS: Arc 410/990, Crest 408/985, Meridian 415/1005, Atlas 430/1030 mm) set its axles (`RA`, `FA`); our wheels, chain, rear derailleur and brakes are built there, the donor is still fitted at the Arc's axles (`RA0`, `FA0`) and its frame stretched to them — the rear triangle back from the seat tube to the dropouts, the front end forward from mid top/down tube to the head tube, linear in x — with the fork and cockpit moved by the front's shift; the donor tires are placed relative to the Arc's axles before going on our wheels. Checked by eye at both dropouts.
- **Frames**: displacement fields on the donor frame, linear across the tubes so straight tubes stay straight: `stack` stretches the head tube up its own axis (bottom and fork crown fixed), lifts the top tube's front end and moves the cockpit with it; `drop` lowers the seat cluster (fading to nothing at the head tube and dropouts), and the seatpost's lower part slides down the seat tube's axis so it still meets the frame. Wheelbase and chainstays stay the Arc's — the donor frame is fitted to fixed axles.
- **Files**: `public/pitch/kord/models/<id>.glb` (~820 KB, ~171k triangles) and `<id>-lite.glb` (~510 KB, ~81k: `--lite 0.45` decimates every part but the decals and thins the tread to 7%); phones (≤899px) load the lite one (`modelUrl()` in bikeScene.js). The old single `bike.glb` is gone. `bikeScene.load(id)` swaps the shown model (the builder and Find your bike switch bikes in place; a newer load wins); `BikeViewer` takes `bike`, the story loads the Arc. Each build's `--stills` renders only its own range still (and the hero poster for the Arc).

**Head badge on long names** (2026-11-24): names over five letters (MERIDIAN) use their initial as a monogram (no ™ on a single letter), and every badge's letters are capped at ARC's 5.5 mm, so a monogram stays a badge.

**The builder's specs on the bike** (2026-11-24, the user's call): choosing a groupset or wheels changes the 3D bike. The groupset's parts — cranks, both derailleurs, both brake calipers — have their own material, `Group` (in build_bike.py and bikeScene.js), whose finish eases between Sport (anodised black), Race (polished gunmetal) and Pro (titanium silver) (`FINISH` in bikeScene.js). The wheels reshape the rims live: `setSpec({ rim })` remaps every rim vertex and the spokes' rim ends from the model's own depth (`RIM_M`, from `wheels` in data.js) to the chosen one — 38 mm light, 62 mm deep, or the bike's own — about the rim's outer edge (`R_OUT` 0.3108 m), eased like the paint; hubs and the cassette (under 0.2 m) are untouched. `BikeViewer` takes `spec`; the builder passes the chosen groupset, rim depth and size. Find your bike's match passes its size too (`spec={{ size }}`), so the 3D match is the rider's size, and its Compare link carries it (`&size=`).

**The frame size on the bike** (2026-11-24, the user's pick of three options: leave it, real geometry, or numbers): choosing a size reshapes the 3D frame live, as real frames differ — the wheels stay 700c, so scaling the whole bike would have been wrong. The model is a 56 (stack 570, saddle 735 mm from the BB along the seat tube); `SIZE_GEO` in data.js gives each size's change in mm — the front end up or down the head tube's axis (`stack`: 49 −42, 52 −28, 54 −14, 58 +16; about three quarters of a real range, since a 49's full −55 squashed the head tube to almost nothing), the seat cluster along the seat tube (`seat`: −55, −35, −18, +18) and the seatpost and saddle (`saddle`: −75, −45, −23, +25). bikeScene's `setSpec({ size })` applies them with the build script's own displacement fields, in its coordinates: the front end fades in over z 0.70–0.80 m and from x −0.10 to 0.30 m (so the fork's crown and the down tube stay), the seat cluster over z 0.42–0.74 m and back from x 0.32 m; the cockpit, seatpost and saddle move whole. Each vertex keeps its three weights (`collectSize`, per model on load, which then applies the current size), so a step is a weighted sum; it eases like the rims. Wheels, cranks, chain, derailleurs and brakes are skipped. Reach (top tube length) doesn't change: it would move the front wheel and the floor shadow, and the height reads clearly on its own. Checked at 49, 54, 56 and 58 on the Arc™, Crest™ and Atlas™ (straight tubes, clean joints), and switching bikes mid-size keeps the size.

**Tablet pass** (2026-11-24, 820×1180 and 1180×820, every page): fixed the range showing one card a row at 700–899px (now two by two), Compare's bike names running together at tablet widths (`clamp(17px, 2.1vw, 24px)`), the builder's faint name running off its stage for long names (sized to the stage with container units, `min(110px, 10.5cqi)`), and — found with the nav change — 900–1100px having no navigation (the menu button now shows wherever the nav is hidden).

## The 3D story (`BikeStory.js`)

One sticky canvas in a 380vh section (320vh on phones). Scroll progress `p` (0–1 over the section) drives it: the hero (copy left, bike right via `setViewOffset`; phones: bike above the copy) fades over 0.02–0.12, the bike turns side-on over 0.06–0.32, explodes over 0.30–0.72 (offsets in `EXPLODE`, x squeezed to 55% on phones; on desktop the exploded bike drops 9% of the screen and its distance also keeps its height within ~57% of the screen, so on 16:9 and wider screens the saddle stays clear of the "Inside the Arc" heading), callouts fade in one by one from 0.42 (the saddle's label hangs left of its dot, `side: "left"`); wheels and cranks roll with the scroll. In the hero it's draggable (inertia; arrow keys on the focusable `.cd-view`; `touch-action: pan-y` keeps vertical swipes scrolling), with a slight pointer parallax, four hotspots (buttons with `aria-expanded` cards) and a paint radio group (roving tabindex). Callouts are DOM labels projected each frame; on phones they shrink to numbered dots with a legend under the bike. `--p`, `--hero` and `data-hero` on the section drive the CSS fades. Look (2026-11-23, after the user found it too shiny): no mirror finishes — each paint in `PAINTS` has its own clear coat (`coat`, `coatRough`; Stealth is satin, the others gloss but not mirror), raw carbon is satin, black alloy parts are anodised (metalness 0.6, roughness 0.52), the gumwall a muted tan; the reflected studio is a few big, soft, dimmer panels blurred by PMREM (sigma 0.1) so reflections are broad sheens, not hard white streaks; exposure 1.25 (NeutralToneMapping). `build_bike.py`'s materials and the stills' paints use the same values (its stills lights were dimmed to suit the rougher surfaces). Realism (2026-11-23, the user asked for all three):
- **Baked ambient occlusion** (`--ao`): Cycles bakes AO (12 cm rays, 128 samples) into each part's per-vertex "AO" attribute. It bakes twice, as is and with every face flipped: a face whose flipped bake is clearly brighter was inside out (our rims, spokes, hubs, rotors, rings, chain, cassette, ~14,600 faces) and is flipped for good, which also fixed their shading on the page; each point keeps the brighter result. Spokes and chain plates have vertices only at their buried ends, so they get a floor (`AO_FLOOR`). It ships as the GLB's only UV channel (u = AO), since this exporter's Draco quantizes UVs but not vertex colors (+26 KB instead of +430 KB); `BikeStory` copies it to an `ao` attribute and applies it in the shader to reflected and ambient light (and half to direct light), fading it to 30% as the bike explodes (it was baked assembled).
- **A real studio** for reflections: `studio.hdr` via `HDRLoader` + PMREM (`ENV_YAW` turns it), awaited before the first frame; the old panel room stays as the fallback. The Blender stills are path-traced (`--pathtrace`, Cycles, 96 samples, denoised) under the same HDRI (1k), with the page's tone mapping (Khronos PBR Neutral, exposure 1.25) and only the chartreuse rim added.
- **Rubber grain**, in the shader (`enhance()` in BikeStory, onBeforeCompile): a 1 mm value-noise grain on `Tire`/`TireWall`/`Gumwall` from the part's own coordinates, faded out where it would be under about a pixel (`fwidth`). A 2×2 twill carbon weave on `Carbon` was tried the same way and removed (the user didn't like it). Not in the stills.
